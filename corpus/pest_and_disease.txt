CROP PEST AND DISEASE ADVISORY (NIGERIAN STAPLE CROPS)

This document supports symptom-based diagnosis: a farmer describes what they observe on their crop in a chat query, and the system matches described symptoms against known disease/pest profiles to advise on likely cause and recommended action. This is a text-based diagnostic aid, not a substitute for physical/visual inspection by an agricultural extension officer for confirmed cases.

CASSAVA MOSAIC DISEASE (CMD): the most severe and widespread cassava disease in Nigeria, endemic across the major cassava-growing belt. Caused by whitefly-transmitted viruses (African cassava mosaic virus and related strains). Symptoms: yellowing and distortion of leaves, dark green blisters/blotchy patterns, leaf chlorosis, vein clearing, and stunted growth. Can cause 20-70% yield loss in susceptible varieties depending on severity, with impact more severe when infection occurs at early growth stage. A large multi-state field survey (Benue, Kogi, Nasarawa, Niger, Plateau, Oyo, Ogun, Ondo, Osun, Ekiti, Kwara, FCT) found substantial CMD presence across surveyed cassava farms in South West and North Central Nigeria, confirming this is a widespread, actively monitored disease across those regions specifically - relevant context given this system's existing state-level focus on those same regions for conflict risk.

CASSAVA BROWN STREAK DISEASE (CBSD): can cause up to 100% yield loss in susceptible varieties by destroying edible roots even when the rest of the plant looks healthy externally - making it a particularly dangerous disease since visual inspection of leaves alone may miss root damage. Symptoms include blotchy yellow or feathery chlorosis on leaf veins, and brown streak-like lesions on young green stems.

CASSAVA ANTHRACNOSE: caused by fungal pathogen (Colletotrichum manihotis). Symptoms: light to dark brown spots on leaves, young stems, and petioles; infected leaves crinkle, dry, wither and fall off.

CASSAVA WHITEFLY (Bemisia tabaci): the primary vector responsible for spreading CMD and CBSD, not just a direct pest itself. Management: early planting at onset of rains, crop rotation, biological control via predatory mites/parasites, and integrated pest management combining multiple approaches rather than single-method control.

YAM ANTHRACNOSE: caused by fungal pathogens including Colletotrichum species. Symptoms: leaf chlorosis, leaf edge necrosis, blights, dark brown to black leaf spots, shot holes, necrotic vein banding and vein browning. Field research in Cross River State's yam belt found this disease prevalent enough to potentially reach epidemic proportions in affected communities, with susceptibility varying significantly by yam variety/landrace - some landraces showed high resistance while others were highly susceptible.

GENERAL DIAGNOSTIC GUIDANCE FOR FARMER-REPORTED SYMPTOMS:
- Yellowing + mosaic/blotchy leaf pattern + distortion -> consider viral disease (CMD-type), especially in cassava; recommend checking for whitefly presence on leaf undersides.
- Brown/dark spots with defined margins, leaves drying and falling -> consider fungal disease (anthracnose-type), common across cassava and yam.
- Yellow chlorosis specifically on leaf veins + stem lesions in cassava, especially with roots that look fine above ground but plant is otherwise stressed -> consider CBSD; recommend root inspection since this disease can be underestimated from leaf symptoms alone.
- Any severe, rapidly spreading, or unfamiliar symptom pattern should be escalated to a local agricultural extension officer for confirmed diagnosis - this system provides preliminary guidance, not definitive diagnosis.

Recommended alert routing: symptom-based crop health queries route to the farm owner with advisory information; queries indicating a potentially severe or spreading outbreak (e.g., multiple plants affected, rapid spread described) should additionally recommend contacting a local agricultural extension officer.

MAIZE FALL ARMYWORM (FAW - Spodoptera frugiperda): first recorded in Nigeria in 2016 (initially observed in the rainforest zone of South-Western Nigeria and at IITA research stations in Ibadan and Ikenne), now a major recurring pest across Nigerian maize production. Symptoms: caterpillars feed on young leaf whorls, ears, and tassels; look for a distinctive "Y"-shaped marking between the eyes on the caterpillar itself; damage includes ragged/window-pane feeding holes in leaves, especially concentrated in the whorl. Late-stage larvae can cut through the base of young seedlings, killing the whole plant. Outbreaks are most severe when the new cropping season follows a period of drought and coincide with the onset of the wet season. Vegetative-stage maize can often recover from moderate FAW damage if growing rapidly, but heavy or late-detected infestations can cause irreversible loss - early detection matters significantly. Management: regular field surveillance (recommended at least a few times per week during risk periods), reporting outbreaks promptly to local agricultural extension contacts, and biological/integrated pest management approaches are increasingly favored over sole reliance on chemical insecticides.

MAIZE STREAK VIRUS (MSV): transmitted by leafhoppers. Symptoms: mottling, chlorotic (yellowish) streaking, and lesions on leaves; white-striped leaf appearance; retarded/stunted plant growth.

MAIZE DOWNY MILDEW: appears as early as two weeks after sowing. Symptoms: chlorosis and stunting; older infected plants show mottling, chlorotic streaking, narrower and more erect leaves than healthy plants, with a white downy growth visible on both leaf surfaces.

MAIZE LETHAL NECROSIS DISEASE (MLND): caused by co-infection of two viruses (Maize Chlorotic Mottle Virus plus a second virus such as Sugarcane Mosaic Virus). Can range from reduced yield to total crop failure - a significant food security concern. Spread by vectors including maize thrips, rootworms, and leaf beetles. Symptoms vary by genotype and infection age but commonly include barren tassels.

FLUTED PUMPKIN (UGWU) - TELFAIRIA MOSAIC VIRUS (TeMV): the most prevalent virus disease of fluted pumpkin in Nigeria. Symptoms: mottling of leaves, low leaf yield, chlorosis, stunting, and abnormal fruit development. Transmitted by aphids (Aphis spiraecola) and also seed-transmitted (meaning infected seed stock can introduce the disease from planting, not just later insect transmission) - sourcing clean seed is therefore a relevant preventive consideration, not just in-season aphid control.

FLUTED PUMPKIN WHITE LEAF SPOT DISEASE: caused by the fungus Phoma sorghina. Reduces leaf lamina (the main leafy portion harvested for consumption, making this directly yield-relevant for a leaf vegetable crop) and can also affect seed quality. Research indicates fluted pumpkin intercropped with maize can significantly reduce leaf spot disease incidence compared to sole-cropping, and application of organic manure (poultry manure, plant ash) has shown measurable reduction in leaf spot severity in field research.

FLUTED PUMPKIN GENERAL PESTS: grasshoppers, beetles, aphids, thrips, and green shield bugs are commonly reported pests affecting ugwu leaves.

Recommended alert routing (updated): symptom-based crop health queries route to the farm owner with advisory information; queries indicating a potentially severe or spreading outbreak (e.g., multiple plants affected, rapid spread described, or a FAW sighting given its documented severity) should additionally recommend contacting a local agricultural extension officer.
