NIGERIA AGROECOLOGICAL ZONE BASELINES (DERIVED REFERENCE FOR DEVIATION DETECTION)

This document provides regional baseline reference ranges for rainfall, vegetation health (NDVI), and growing conditions across Nigeria's major agroecological zones, derived from published climatological and remote-sensing research. It is intended as a static, offline comparison reference: when a local IoT soil/environmental sensor reading is available, it can be compared against the expected baseline for that zone and season to flag meaningful deviations (e.g., "expected soil moisture for this zone in August is elevated due to peak rains; your sensor reads low - possible localized drainage or sensor issue worth investigating").

NIGERIA'S SIX MAJOR AGROECOLOGICAL/VEGETATION ZONES (south to north):
1. Mangrove/Freshwater Swamp (coastal Niger Delta): highest rainfall zone, above 3500mm/year in coastal areas. Brackish/marshy conditions; fishing and aquaculture dominate over crop farming.
2. Rainforest: ultra-humid, above 2000mm/year rainfall, monthly temperatures 23-33C. NDVI values in this zone are the highest nationally (research sampling found Bayelsa State, within this zone, showing mean NDVI around 0.53 - the highest of sampled states, reflecting dense vegetation cover).
3. Guinea Savanna: sub-humid, above 1000mm/year rainfall, 20-37C. The most extensive ecological zone, covering more than half of Nigeria's land area. Supports intensive agriculture including maize, yam, and diverse staple crops. Research sampling found Benue State (within this zone) showing mean NDVI around 0.44.
4. Sudan Savanna: semi-arid, 440-1000mm/year rainfall (estimates vary by source; generally 700-1000mm in the core zone), 13-40C, high rainfall variability and frequent drought, poor soil fertility a major agricultural constraint. Highest cattle concentration in the country. Major crops: groundnuts, cotton, millet, maize. Research sampling found Kaduna State (within/near this zone) showing mean NDVI around 0.39.
5. Sahel Savanna: semi-arid to arid, 440-600mm/year rainfall (lowest of the savanna zones), 13-40C, found in extreme northwest and northeast. Transitional zone between savanna and Sahara. Drought-tolerant crops only (pearl millet, cowpea, groundnut).
6. Derived Savanna (transition zone between rainforest and Guinea savanna): average annual rainfall around 1314mm, average temperature around 26.5C.

SEASONAL PATTERN ACROSS ZONES: in most regions the rainy season runs March/April to October, with the southern part having bimodal rainfall distribution; the dry season (Harmattan) runs November to March. This aligns with the planting/harvest seasonal risk patterns described elsewhere in this corpus - deviation-checking is most useful during the rainy season when moisture-dependent crops are most vulnerable to shortfall, and during dry season for irrigation-dependent crops (tomato, ugwu).

HOW TO USE THIS BASELINE: this data provides expected RANGES, not precise real-time satellite readings for a specific field on a specific day - actual Sentinel-2/NDVI integration for live per-field monitoring is a documented future work item, not part of the current offline system. The value of this reference is enabling qualitative reasoning: comparing a live sensor reading against the zone's known seasonal norm to flag whether a reading looks unusually dry, unusually wet, or within expected range for the time of year and location.

Sources referenced: land transformation and agroecological zone research (Iloeje 2001; Adelodun, Choi, and Echodu 2018); Nigeria eco-climatic zone rainfall monitoring research; NDVI ecological zone sampling research (Bayelsa/Benue/Kaduna comparative study); Agriculture Nigeria agroecological zone reference.
