NIGERIA STATE-LEVEL FARMER-HERDER CONFLICT RISK TIERS AND GPS REFERENCE

This document provides approximate GPS coordinates (state capital, for general geolocation reference) and a risk tier classification for all 36 Nigerian states plus the Federal Capital Territory. Risk tiers are based on documented conflict research concentration, not precise incident counts, since exhaustive state-by-state incident data is not consistently available. Lower tier does not mean zero risk — it reflects less concentrated documentation in available research.

TIER 1 - HIGH DOCUMENTED RISK (part of the 7 states research identifies as accounting for the large majority of recorded farmer-herder conflict incidents and deaths nationally):
- Benue State (capital Makurdi, approx 7.73N, 8.52E) - North Central. Highest-casualty state in most tracking data. Documented primary causes: water source contamination by cattle, crop destruction, grazing on disputed fallow land.
- Plateau State (capital Jos, approx 9.92N, 8.90E) - North Central. History of severe mass-casualty incidents; research shows local governance/mediation quality strongly affects escalation outcomes here.
- Nasarawa State (capital Lafia, approx 8.49N, 8.52E) - North Central. Frequent spillover from Benue State conflict; documented large-scale displacement events.
- Kaduna State (capital Kaduna, approx 10.52N, 7.44E) - North West. Conflict intensity has fluctuated significantly over time (elevated 2012-13, reduced 2013-19, re-emerged 2020 onward); Southern Kaduna particularly affected.
- Taraba State (capital Jalingo, approx 8.89N, 11.36E) - North East. Site of some of the most severe recorded single incidents (Mambilla Plateau area).
- Adamawa State (capital Yola, approx 9.20N, 12.48E) - North East.
- Kogi State (capital Lokoja, approx 7.80N, 6.74E) - North Central.

TIER 2 - DOCUMENTED MODERATE/EMERGING RISK (confirmed recurring incidents in recent research and news reporting, though less concentrated in long-run academic tracking than Tier 1):
- Enugu State (capital Enugu, approx 6.44N, 7.50E) - South East. Documented recurring incidents, notably concentrated around harvest season (reported pattern: attacks occur "every year during harvest" in affected communities per 2025 reporting).
- Ebonyi State (capital Abakaliki, approx 6.32N, 8.11E) - South East.
- Ondo State (capital Akure, approx 7.25N, 5.20E) - South West.
- Oyo State (capital Ibadan, approx 7.38N, 3.90E) - South West, including the Ibarapa area which has recorded multiple documented incidents.
- Niger State (capital Minna, approx 9.61N, 6.56E) - North Central.
- Zamfara State (capital Gusau, approx 12.17N, 6.66E) - North West. Conflict here is also heavily intertwined with the broader Nigerian bandit conflict.
- Katsina State (capital Katsina, approx 12.99N, 7.60E) - North West.

TIER 3 - LOWER DOCUMENTED INCIDENCE (limited concentrated documentation in available research; general regional baseline risk applies; monitoring is still recommended, particularly during peak seasonal migration windows):
- Abuja / FCT (approx 9.08N, 7.40E) - Central, bordered by Tier 1/2 states.
- Sokoto (13.06N, 5.24E), Kebbi (12.45N, 4.20E), Jigawa (12.23N, 9.56E), Kano (12.00N, 8.52E), Bauchi (10.31N, 9.85E), Gombe (10.29N, 11.17E), Borno (11.85N, 13.16E), Yobe (11.75N, 11.97E) - North West / North East. Some overlap with broader insurgency-related instability; herder migration routes pass through these states seasonally.
- Kwara (8.50N, 4.55E), Federal-adjacent North Central fringe states.
- Anambra (6.21N, 7.07E), Imo (5.49N, 7.03E), Abia (5.53N, 7.49E) - South East, lower documented incidence than Enugu/Ebonyi but geographically proximate.
- Delta (5.89N, 5.68E), Edo (6.34N, 5.62E), Rivers (4.84N, 6.99E), Bayelsa (4.92N, 6.26E), Cross River (5.97N, 8.32E), Akwa Ibom (5.02N, 7.92E) - South South. Lowest documented farmer-herder incidence nationally; other land-use conflicts (oil, fishing rights) are more commonly reported in this zone.
- Lagos (6.52N, 3.38E), Ogun (7.16N, 3.35E), Osun (7.77N, 4.56E), Ekiti (7.72N, 5.31E) - South West. Ekiti has passed anti-open-grazing legislation specifically in response to documented incidents.

SEASONAL RISK PATTERN (applies as a modifier across all states/tiers, based on climate-migration research):
- Dry season (approximately November-March): herders migrate further south and toward river valleys/water sources in search of pasture and water as northern grazing land dries out. Contact risk with settled farming communities increases as migration routes cross into or near farmland.
- Onset of rains / planting season (approximately April-June): newly planted, vulnerable young crops increase the economic stakes of any livestock incursion, raising the severity of conflict when incidents occur even if frequency is not yet peak.
- Growing season (approximately July-September): crops are established; incursions cause substantial and visible damage, a well-documented trigger point for confrontation.
- Harvest season (approximately September-November): documented as a specifically elevated-risk window in multiple affected communities (Enugu State reporting specifically identifies harvest season as a recurring annual attack pattern), likely because harvested/stored crops and cleared fields intersect with herd movement as herders begin returning toward drier grazing areas or repositioning for the dry season ahead.

How to use this for risk scoring: combine the state's tier (baseline risk level) with the current seasonal window (modifier) to produce a combined risk estimate — e.g., a Tier 1 state during harvest season represents peak combined risk; a Tier 3 state during the dry-season off-migration window represents baseline lower risk. This is a reasoned combination of two independently documented factors, not a precise statistical prediction.

Sources referenced: ACCORD conflict pattern analysis (7-state concentration data); state-specific academic and news research cited in the conflict_patterns.txt document in this corpus; Punch Nigeria reporting on Enugu State harvest-season attack patterns (2025); general climate-migration conflict research on Sahel-to-Middle-Belt herd movement patterns. GPS coordinates are approximate state capital locations for general reference only, not precise incident locations.
